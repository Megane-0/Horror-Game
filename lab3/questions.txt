How did you handle the camera matrix for the skybox?
- We zeroed out the translation components when drawing the skybox

How did you represent the objects? Is this a good way to manage a scene or would you do it differently for a "real" application?
- We modified the model-to-world matrix for each drawn model. In a real application one would like to do it all once before display is called.

What special considerations are needed when rendering a skybox?
- Turn of z buffering and have the camera inside it. It should rotate with the camera but not translate.

Where is the camera in view coordinates?
- its at (0,0,0)

Why was blue facing the camera when visualizing the normal vectors?
- Because of the the normal facing the camera is ~(0,0,1) which mapped to a color becomes blue.

For light source 3, why did we get a white area in the distance for the specular light but not for the diffuse?
- The specular light takes into account the viewing direction and far away it becomes almost parallell to the reflection direction and thus the dot(R,V) ~ 1 meaning strong light.
- Diffuse light doesn't take into account the viewing direction and thus doesn't have this property.

How do you generate a vector from the surface to the eye?
- simply take -surfacepoint, since in viewspace eye is at (0,0,0), should probably normalize as well.

Which vectors need renormalization in the fragment shader?
- Vectors that are interpolated, such as normal and viewdirection but also lightdirection.
